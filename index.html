Comprehensive First Aid Guide - Lecture 1

Everything you need to understand primary survey, recovery position, and CPR simply

First: Who is a good paramedic and what are their principles?

To be a successful paramedic and save a life, there are basic things to remember with the word D-R-E-C-N-T:

  • Do your best: Making maximum effort to help the injured.
  • Respect: Without any discrimination between people.
  • Explain: Tell the injured and those around you what you plan to do to reassure them.
  • Consent: If a rational person is conscious, you must ask permission first, but if unconscious or a child under 12, permission is not needed.
  • Never leave: Stay by their side until the ambulance arrives.
  • Trained only: Do not do something you haven't been trained to do so you don't harm the injured.

Classification of injured by age:

  • Infant: From birth to 1 year.
  • Child: From 1 year to puberty.
  • Adult: From puberty onwards.

Second: Primary Survey (Check – Call – Care)

As soon as you see an accident or a sick person, you follow this exact order:

1. Check the scene and person (CHECK)

Scene: Make sure the place is safe for you, the injured, and those around you.

Person: Tap his shoulder gently, give a light shake, and speak loudly: "Sir, are you okay?"

Level of consciousness: Conscious (responds fully), Semi-conscious (opens eyes but unfocused and needs urgent help), Unconscious completely.

2. Call for help (CALL)

In Egypt, the emergency ambulance number is 123.

When you call them, tell them: Exact location, number of injured, what happened, whether anyone is giving first aid, and ask for an AED if available. Never hang up until the operator tells you to!

3. Provide care and assessment (CARE)

Breathing: Check the injured person's breathing in a maximum of 5 seconds (gasping or sudden sighing is not normal breathing!).

Pulse: If not breathing, check the pulse for 5 to 10 seconds (from carotid artery for adults, brachial artery for infants).

Open airway: By head-tilt/chin-lift, and if neck injury is suspected, use jaw thrust.

Third: Recovery Position

If you find an unconscious person who is breathing and has a normal pulse, lay them on their side (recovery position).

Why do we do this? So they don't swallow their tongue and so if they vomit or fluids come out, they exit without blocking the throat and suffocating them. For infants, we hold them on our arm with the head slightly lowered and supported.

Fourth: Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR)

When the heart stops working and breathing stops (sudden cardiac arrest), brain cells begin to be affected and die after just 3 minutes!

CPR Comparison Table (Adult, Child, Infant):

Comparison Adult Child Infant
Depth Not less than 5 cm About 5 cm About 4 cm
Speed More than 100/min More than 100/min More than 100/min
Ratio (1 rescuer) 30 : 2 30 : 2 30 : 2
Ratio (2 rescuers) 30 : 2 15 : 2 15 : 2
Compression method Palms of hands on top of each other One palm (or two) Two fingers only

When to stop CPR?

  • Normal breathing has returned to the person.
  • You are exhausted and unable to continue.
  • The Automated External Defibrillator (AED) arrived and is ready.
  • Ambulance arrived and took over the case.
  • The place became very dangerous for you.

Fifth: Practical Scenarios and Cases (1 - 4)

Case 1: Someone fell off a bike and their leg is bleeding

Solution: Is the place safe? Yes. Conscious? Yes, communicating. We get permission, examine from top to bottom, treat severe bleeding immediately to prevent shock and call ambulance.

Case 2: Someone lying on the ground in camp unresponsive but their chest is rising and falling

Solution: This means they are unconscious but breathing! Do not do CPR, place them immediately in recovery position and send someone to call 123 ambulance.

Case 3: Someone fell suddenly, making gasping sounds, not breathing, and pulse is absent

Solution: This is clear cardiac arrest! Immediately start CPR (chest compressions 5 cm deep at 30:2) and send someone to get AED and call ambulance.

Case 4: An 8-month-old baby unresponsive, gasping, and weak pulse below 50

Solution: We check brachial pulse, if weak or absent we start infant CPR with 2 fingers 4 cm deep at 30:2 (one rescuer) or 15:2 (two rescuers).
